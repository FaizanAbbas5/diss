The aim is to determine whether a learned or engineered representation of tabular data, provided to a frozen LLM, improves faithfulness relative to a raw-table baseline. Three architectures are compared where the third is an optional extension.

Baseline (raw table to LLM). The serialised table is supplied directly to a frozen, prompt-based LLM. Strong iterative prompting strategies such as chain-of-density will be used to ensure a competitive baseline.
Learned representation to adapter to LLM. A neural encoder (for example a Neural Additive Model or a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) trained as a classifier or regressor) produces a learned representation of the table. As this representation cannot be supplied to a frozen LLM directly, a LoRA-style adapter is trained to project it into the model's token-embedding space (inspired by the encoder-adapter design of vision-language models). However, If adapter training becomes infeasible due to the available compute or time constraints, the representation will instead be transformed to text through deterministic templates (for example using Arria NLG or Jinja), which introduce no hallucinations of their own.
Engineered representation to adapter to LLM (optional). Similar to (2), but with hand-crafted features instead of the learned encoder, allowing a controlled comparison of learned versus manual representations and an ablation study. Undertaken only if (1) and (2) are completed with time remaining.
